The Acrophonic Principle

The acrophonic principle connects a written sign to the first sound of a word chosen to represent it. In this chart, each Hachukma letter is paired with an example word whose beginning sound matches that letter. For example, the letter for /a/ is shown with ang, which begins with the sound /a/. These examples help show how the letters relate to the sounds they represent.

About the /e/ and /i/ letters

  • The Hachukma letter labeled /e/ is shown with imang, whose first sound is /e/. 
  • The Hachukma letter labeled /i/ is shown with aikha, whose first sound is /ai/. 

Acrophonic exceptions and flexibility

Most Hachukma examples relate to the first sound of the word, but a few use another sound from the word instead:

  • Wasung (bamboo tube) represents /ŋ/, heard at the end of the word rather than its initial /w/. 
  • rə (to give) represents the vowel /ə/, rather than the word's initial /r/. 

These examples are intentional variations in how the acrophonic principle is applied.
